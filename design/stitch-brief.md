Design a high-quality, industry-standard marketing landing page (desktop and mobile) for **Fixth**. The current live version is at https://fixth-frontend-pkzd.vercel.app/. Treat it only as a reference for the brand colours and logo. Do not copy its layout, structure or styling, and do not feel bound by it.

Everything below is context: facts, content and constraints you can draw from. It is not a section list or a wireframe. You decide the structure, the order, what to emphasise, what to leave out and how it should look. Pick the content that makes the strongest page.

## 1. What Fixth is

Fixth is a career platform built only for engineering. It connects **verified engineering students in Nigeria** with **verified engineering companies** for:

- **SIWES placements** (Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme): the industrial attachment Nigerian engineering students must complete as part of their degree.
- **Industrial Training (IT)**: structured on-site training programmes at engineering firms.
- **Internships**: paid or unpaid, with stipend, duration and location listed upfront.
- **Graduate and entry-level roles** (coming later): for fresh graduates and NYSC corps members.

Product vision: *to be the most trusted platform connecting engineering students with engineering companies for SIWES, industrial training, internships, and future graduate opportunities.*

How it positions itself to companies: a **"campus recruitment extension"** and **specialised talent infrastructure** that gives firms a pre-vetted pipeline of engineering students and recent graduates. Unlike general job boards, it is only for engineering talent.

Platform: a responsive web app (a mobile app is planned for later). Most students will visit on their phones.

---

## 2. The problem it solves

**For students**
- SIWES and internship openings are scattered across WhatsApp groups, personal networks, lecturers, notice boards and individual company websites. Miss one message and you miss the role.
- Many talented students never get a relevant placement because they aren't visible to companies. Some end up in placements unrelated to their field, which widens the skills gap.
- There's no way to tell whether a company or a listing is real.
- Applications vanish into a "black hole": no status, no reply, no closure.
- Getting a placement often depends on **who you know**, not on merit.

**For companies**
- It's hard to find qualified engineering students, and the applications that arrive are unstructured.
- There's no reliable way to verify a student's eligibility or identity beforehand, so firms are cautious about taking on unverified talent.
- Hiring is slow, expensive and often relies on referrals and nepotism rather than merit.
- Fresh graduates often lack the "market readiness" firms need.

**The core idea:** a single, trusted, structured place where verified students meet verified companies, and placements are earned on merit.

---

## 3. Audiences

1. **Primary: Nigerian engineering undergraduates**, roughly 18–25, usually 300–400 level, from universities and polytechnics. They are often under pressure because SIWES is mandatory. Most browse on mobile and are price-sensitive. They want **trust** (is this real?), **relevance** (does it match my discipline?) and **visibility** (where does my application stand?).
2. **Secondary: engineering companies and their HR or recruiting teams.** Examples are firms in oil and gas, power, construction, manufacturing, steel, telecoms and aviation maintenance. They want a fast, low-effort way to hire screened, eligible students.
3. **Later: recent graduates and NYSC corps members** looking for graduate trainee and entry-level roles.

The page has to work for students first while giving companies a clear, credible path of their own.

## 4. What students can do (content to draw from)

- **Create a verified profile.** Sign up with a school email (or a personal email plus student ID and matric number, verified manually). Accept the Terms and Privacy Policy.
- **Pass two-step identity verification:**
  1. Proof of academic status: an official school ID card or a screenshot of the student portal showing active registration.
  2. A live identity check: a real-time liveness photo matched against the documents, which stops fake profiles.
  Status shows as *Unverified → Pending verification → Verified*. Students can browse while unverified; **applying unlocks once they're verified**.
- **Set academic details:** institution, department/discipline, academic level (chosen from lists).
- **Set placement preferences:** current location, preferred placement location(s), start date and duration. Example: currently in Ondo State, wants Lagos or Abuja, available from June 2027 for 6 months.
- **Build a showcase profile:** skills (from a discipline-based list plus custom skills), an about/bio section, a professional headshot, projects with images and links (GitHub, Google Drive, Figma, live demo), and a location-privacy toggle. They can preview the profile exactly as recruiters see it.
- **Upload one active CV** (PDF or DOC) and replace it at any time.
- **Discover placements:** browse and search listings, **filtered by discipline, location and duration**. Only listings from verified companies appear. Listings are ranked by relevance to discipline, level and preferred location.
- **Apply in one step** with the Fixth profile and CV. **No cover letter.** Students can only apply to roles open to their discipline.
- **Track every application** through clear stages: **Submitted → Viewed → Shortlisted → Accepted / Rejected**.
- **Read receipts:** students are notified the moment a recruiter opens their application. This directly answers the "black hole" anxiety.
- **Notifications** for every verification and application status change.
- Withdraw an application, and delete the account and data permanently.
- Progress is shown as milestones rather than a percentage: *Starter Profile → Verified Student → Recruiter-Ready* (headshot, bio, at least 3 skills, at least 1 project).

## 5. What companies can do (content to draw from)

- **Create a company profile:** name, industry, description, office location(s), logo, website and social links.
- **Get verified:** submit the **CAC registration** (Corporate Affairs Commission) number. The Fixth team reviews the company before any listing goes live, and companies are told the result and the reason.
- **Post placements** with role title, description, eligible engineering discipline(s), required academic level, location, duration and stipend information. Each listing has a **unique shareable application link**.
- **Receive only eligible applicants**, restricted by discipline and level.
- **Review applicants** with their verified badge, profile, skills, projects and CV.
- **Filter applicants** by discipline, academic level and location.
- **Update status in one click** (Viewed, Shortlisted, Rejected, Accepted). The student sees it immediately, so recruiters aren't chased for updates.
- Contact shortlisted candidates through Fixth or via email/WhatsApp handoff (coming later).
- Close or expire listings and keep the application history.
- **Pricing: free for companies during the launch phase.**
- **Coming soon: AI-powered screening.** AI reads the job description and each CV and profile, gives every applicant a **0–100% match score with a plain explanation**, and groups applicants into high, potential and low match. **Humans always make the final decision; the AI never rejects anyone automatically.**

## 6. Trust and safety (central to the brand)

Trust is Fixth's main point of difference. Content to draw from:

- **Verified on both sides.** Every student is verified (school email or ID, plus a liveness check) and every company is verified (CAC number plus review by the Fixth team).
- Only verified companies can publish listings visible to students.
- Verified badges are shown to both sides.
- Admins can unpublish suspicious listings and suspend student or company accounts.
- Students will be able to report suspicious listings (planned).

## 8. FAQ material

- **Is Fixth free?** Companies can post and recruit for free during launch. *(Student pricing isn't confirmed in our docs; use a placeholder or "Free for students" marked TBC.)*
- **How does student verification work?** Confirm your school email, upload your student ID or a portal screenshot, then take a quick live photo. You can browse while we review; applying unlocks once you're verified.
- **I don't have a school email. Can I still join?** Yes. Sign up with a personal email and submit your student ID and matric number for manual verification.
- **How are companies verified?** Every company submits its CAC registration and is reviewed by the Fixth team. Its listings stay hidden until it's approved.
- **Do I need a cover letter?** No. Your Fixth profile and CV are your application, sent in one step.
- **Will I know if a company has seen my application?** Yes. You're notified when it's viewed and at every status change.
- **Which disciplines and locations are covered?** All engineering disciplines, with placements across Nigeria.
- **Are graduate roles available?** Graduate trainee and entry-level roles for fresh graduates and NYSC members are coming after launch.
- **(Companies) Can I limit who applies?** Yes. Restrict each listing by discipline and academic level.

## 10. Brand constraints (must follow)

- **Logo:** keep the existing lowercase **"fixth"** wordmark exactly as it is (it's on the live site). It is used in **blue `#0F35DB`** on light backgrounds and **white** on dark ones. Don't redraw, restyle or replace it.
- **Colours: keep the brand palette.** How you use and balance it is up to you.
  - Brand blue `#0F35DB` (primary) and a darker blue `#0C2AAE`
  - Navy `#00002E` (deep ground / ink)
  - Cyan `#BCF7FB` and soft cyan `#D5FFFF` (accents)
  - Off-white `#F3F3F3` and white `#FFFFFF`
  You can add neutral greys for text and lines. Don't introduce a new hue as a brand colour.
- **Type:** the current site uses Poppins (display) and Instrument Sans (body). You can keep or change the typefaces if it improves the design.
- **Accessibility:** text contrast at least 4.5:1, touch targets at least 44px, a readable 16px+ body on mobile.

## 11. Tone of voice

Confident, clear, encouraging and credible, like a trusted senior engineer, not a hype-driven startup. Plain English and short sentences. Speak to students' real situation (SIWES pressure, uncertainty, "who you know") without being condescending. To companies, sound efficient and professional. Use local terms naturally: SIWES, IT, ITF, CAC, NYSC, matric number, 300L/400L, logbook.

Message themes you can use or rework (they don't have to be used verbatim):
- Placements earned on merit, not on who you know.
- Verified on both sides.
- Apply once, track everything.
- One profile, every placement type.
- Know where you stand / no more application black holes.
- Your campus recruitment team, without the campus tour (for companies).
- Built only for engineering.

## 12. Designing for Nigeria (a core principle, not decoration)

Fixth's audience is Nigerian, and the page should feel unmistakably made for Nigerian engineering students and companies. That has to come through **specifics**, not stereotypes. A Nigerian student should feel "this was built for me", not "this is a global template with a flag on it".

**Avoid clichés:** green-white-green colour schemes, Ankara or adire patterns used as filler, maps of Africa, Pidgin slogans as headlines, or "Naija" gimmicks. The brand colours stay as defined in section 10.

**Language**
- Nigerian English with **British spelling** (programme, organisation, enrolment, centre, licence).
- Use local terms naturally and without explaining them: SIWES, IT, ITF, CAC, NYSC, corper, matric number, 100L–500L, HOD, departmental SIWES coordinator, logbook, placement letter, acceptance letter.
- Tone: warm, direct and respectful, like a sharp older sibling or senior colleague who has already been through SIWES. Not a stiff corporate HR page and not hype-driven American startup speak.
- Keep Pidgin out of the main copy. At most one light, playful touch, never in key messages.

**Name the pain students recognise**
- "Begging for a placement letter", chasing the HOD or lecturers for contacts, scrolling WhatsApp groups for openings, "who you know", the uncertainty of not hearing back.
- Academic calendars shift because of strikes and delayed sessions, so flexible start dates and availability windows are worth presenting as a benefit.

**Local trust signals**
- Placement scams are a real problem: fake offers that charge "processing" or "placement fees". A clear statement such as *"Fixth will never ask you to pay for a placement"* would be a strong trust message. *(The client must confirm this is their policy; show it as a placeholder if unconfirmed.)*
- Company verification through the **CAC** (Corporate Affairs Commission) means something specific to Nigerian readers. Use the acronym confidently.
- Show recognisable school names in mockups (for example UNILAG, FUTA, ABU Zaria, UNN, OAU, UNIBEN, Covenant, Yabatech) as examples of where students come from. Don't present them as partners.

**Realistic local detail in mockups and examples**
- Nigerian names across regions and genders (for example Chiamaka Okafor, Tunde Adeyemi, Aisha Bello, Emeka Nwosu, Zainab Yusuf, Ifeoluwa Ojo).
- Stipends in **Naira (₦)**, for example "₦50,000/month", marked as illustrative.
- Real cities and states (Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan, Kano, Kaduna, Warri, Enugu, Ogun) and real industries (oil and gas, power, construction, manufacturing, telecoms, steel, aviation maintenance).
- Phone format +234, dates as DD/MM/YYYY.

**Built for how Nigerians actually browse**
- **Mobile-first** for mid-range Android phones, often on expensive or patchy mobile data. Keep pages light: optimised images, no autoplaying heavy video, and nothing essential hidden behind heavy animation.
- **WhatsApp is the default channel.** Consider "Share on WhatsApp" for listings and WhatsApp as a support or contact option.
- Large, clear tap targets and readable type in bright outdoor light.

## 13. Imagery direction

- Show real **Nigerian engineering contexts**: students and young engineers on sites, in workshops, plants, labs and hangars, wearing PPE and hard hats. Show both women and men, with diverse settings across Nigeria.
- Show the **product itself**: listing cards, the verification badge, the application status tracker with read receipts, the company applicant view with filters and (coming-soon) match scores. Product UI is the strongest proof.
- **Avoid:** generic Western stock photography, placeholder people ("John Doe"), staged video-call stock images, abstract blobs and decorative gradients with no meaning.

## 14. Honesty rules (important)

Fixth is pre-launch or early-stage. **Do not invent:**
- user numbers ("thousands of students"), placement counts or success rates
- testimonials, quotes, reviews or success stories
- partner, client or university logos
- press mentions or awards

Where proof would normally go, use the **verification process, the product UI and the real features** instead, or clearly marked placeholders (for example `[Partner logo]`, `[Stat]`). Label future features such as AI screening, graduate roles, the mobile app and in-app messaging as **"Coming soon"**.

## 15. What to avoid (lessons from the current page)

The current page was judged generic and low on trust. It uses a template-like dark navy hero with a glow, the headline "LETS GET YOU YOUR NEXT INTERNSHIP ROLE" (missing apostrophe), placeholder names, duplicated listing cards, an unsupported "join thousands" claim, a thin story with no problem, process or proof, almost nothing for companies, and a mobile layout that just stacks the desktop one. Avoid all of these.

## 16. Deliverables

- A complete landing page design for **desktop (1440px)** and **mobile (390px)**. The mobile version should be designed in its own right, not simply stacked.
- It must serve **students** first and give **companies** a clear, credible path.
- The first screen should make it obvious within five seconds what Fixth is, who it's for and what to do next.
- Aim for a distinctive, premium and modern look that feels specific to Nigerian engineering students and companies (see section 12), not a generic SaaS template. The structure, sections, layout, interactions and visual style are entirely yours to decide.
